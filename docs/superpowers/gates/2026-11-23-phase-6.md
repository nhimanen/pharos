## Fixes found by the gate

Each fix has a test that fails without it (mutation-checked).

1. **ba91963: the reshard lease expired during every build longer than the lease TTL.**
   - The per-project reshard lease was renewed only by the work-loop tick, and a reshard build runs inside a tick. A build longer than 30 s (the default TTL) therefore lost the lease at its first publish check: `Reshard of project lucene to spec version 1 stopped: the lease of writer gate-w1 on shard lucene expired`. The step then ran again from the finished local build.
   - In run 1 this happened to the lucene ring-4 reshard (4 min 40 s build) and to the pharos module reshard (46 s).
   - The fix: the work loop starts the reshard lease manager's renewer with its own.
   - Test: `ReshardLeaseRenewalTest`.
2. **d6835f8: a reshard left the moved files' deleted documents in the shards that lost them.**
   - Lucene counts deleted documents in `docFreq` and `docCount` until a merge drops them. After 4 → 5, s01, s03 and s04 held 13–21 % deleted documents, and s01's body docCount was still 18 996 with 15 088 live documents. Every moved file was counted twice in the global BM25 statistics, every score shifted, and keyword top-10 matched before on only 66 of 99 queries.
   - The fix: the reshard applies its deletes (an NRT reader; a flush alone does not) and merges them away (`forceMergeDeletes`, 0 % allowed) in the shards it writes, before the commit. Shards it does not touch stay byte-identical.
   - Tests:
     - `ShardedWriterExpungeTest`, with 12.5 % deletes, below the merge policy's natural 20 % threshold;
     - `ReshardJobTest.ringChangeLeavesNoDeletedDocumentsInTheShardsItRewrites`. Its tiny fixture is merged naturally, so it does not fail without the fix; the unit test does.
3. **0d0ac43: a reshard taken over at another checkout path stalled.**
   - The takeover writer restored the index built under the dead writer's checkout. The step then logged `its local index needs a full run first` on every poll, 68 times over 11 min in run 1, and nothing scheduled that run, because the source had not changed.
   - The branch for a restore that needs a full run had the same flaw: it set `upgrade`, which the next poll recomputed and cleared.
   - The fix: the step records why a full run must come first (`fullRunFirst`). The poll's upgrade reason keeps the project pending until a full run commits.
   - Test: `ReshardJobTest.takeoverAtAnotherCheckoutPathRunsTheFullRunAndFinishesTheReshard`, with a git source and two writers with their own work dirs.
   - Production writers sharing one work-dir path (containers) would not hit it. Single-host clusters and writers with different `workDir`s do.

## Round 2 (rulings P6-R16 and P6-R17, keyword drift root cause)

### Keyword drift (2d): root cause and fix (c82f931)

Debugged by comparing split against unsplit **through the same coordinator and fan-out path**, not against a local search, on `SplitProjectSearchTest`'s cluster (the pharos corpus whole and in three ring shards) with the gate's own mined `synonyms.txt` (41 061 lines).

- **Statistics are not the cause.** On a differing query ("DocumentMapper"), the raw BM25 scores of the whole index, a MultiReader over the three split shards and a per-shard `GlobalStatsSearcher` fan-out were bitwise equal at every rank. The fan-out recorded 0 `GlobalStatsSearcher` fallbacks per shard; all 13 query terms, synonym expansions included, were shipped.
- **Only the tie group at the cut differed.** Ranks 6–10 shared one raw score (9.711756), and Lucene breaks ties by doc id. Doc ids depend on the build: parallel writes, segments, and how a project is split. So the two builds kept different documents of the group.
- **The graph boost turned that into score drift.** The shaped score multiplies by `1 + 0.3·log1p(inDegree)/log1p(maxInDegree)`, with maxInDegree taken over the candidate page. A different tied document at the cut changes maxInDegree and rescales every score on the page. That matches the gate: score-bitwise 0, ratios 0.9995–1.002, about a third exact (pages whose maximum did not change), and reorderings.
- **Was the measurement wrong? No.** "Before" and "after" were different index builds, so any two builds of the same source, resharded or not, could differ this way. The Phase 3 bar (98% identical top-10) stands, so the ranking itself was made build-independent.
- **Fix.**
  - Keyword retrieval reads the whole tie group at the cut (up to 10 000 hits; keys from four stored fields) and ranks equal raw scores by `(id, docType, startLine, endLine)`.
  - The coordinator's keyword merge breaks ties by the same key, so a single node and a fan-out stay bitwise equal.
  - `SplitProjectSearchTest` now requires split and unsplit builds to be bitwise equal for every query, ties at the cut included (it fails without the fix).
  - The parity fixture's local results were re-recorded, and the new fixture passes against the old index build.

### P6-R16: the coordinator re-plans once (f81f6ff)

- A split shard whose reader swapped generations is first retried on another reader at the pinned generation (statistics unchanged).
- Otherwise, after a "generation changed during the query" or a synonyms mismatch, the coordinator waits at most 2 s until its view of the query's shards (readers' generations, catalog pins) or its synonyms has moved on. It then plans the query again and runs it once with new term statistics.
- If the second run still loses shards, it returns that partial answer with its warnings, as before.
- Tests: `ReplanTest`.
  - A reader activates the next run between the statistics and search rounds.
  - A synonyms publish reaches the coordinator late.
  - Both answer complete without a warning, and both fail with the re-plan turned off.

### P6-R17: vector parity after a resplit is judged by recall

Run 3's vector data, re-judged offline: mean overlap 0.9970, shared scores all bitwise equal, but 1 query below 90% (`TieredMergePolicy`, 0.8: 2 documents swapped). The re-run measures it again.

**2c, the 340 s failures.**
- These are the same flip-time generation switch as in run 3. The re-plan ran but did not mend them.
- Its wait is at most 2 s, or half the remaining budget, while the gate's readers heartbeat every 5 s (`heartbeatSeconds` 5). So the re-planned query routed on the same view and failed again, and the first, partial answer was returned (fix 6a1d91c).
- Proposal, not done: on "generation changed", the coordinator could re-pin the swapped shard to the generation the reader reported. That is safe only when the reader serves the new version's whole set. Alternatively, readers could keep answering the previous generation of a rewritten shard for a grace period after they switch.

**2c / 4a, the 560 s failures (a new finding, not fixed).**
- After the 4 → 5 flip, the catalog pins the active version's generations, which equal `LATEST` once the flip has finished.
- The takeover writer's full run (fix 3) published generation 3 of every ring-5 shard. The publish commits every `LATEST` first, backs up the writer state, and only then clears the pins (`ObjectStorePublisher`, step 7).
- Readers follow `LATEST` and activated run 3 as a set. Coordinators routed the still-pinned generation 2 until the pins were cleared and their cached catalogs refreshed: about 10 s in which every lucene shard was left out.
- Proposal: when the active pins equal the current `LATEST` (always true after the flip finished), the first run clears them before its commits and waits a few catalog-cache periods, as the flip's `minPinned` does. Or readers keep serving a pinned generation until the pins are cleared. Either is a design choice for Task 4's pinning.

- This is the page-normalised graph boost: the new document raises the page's highest in-degree, which lowers every other document's boost.
- Raw scores are neither in the search API's results nor in the trace, so I could not show raw-score equality directly. Shared documents in the 98 queries with identical ids are bitwise equal.
- Ruling P6-R17's "bitwise shared scores" therefore cannot hold whenever recall swaps a document with a higher in-degree into a page. Comparing raw scores would need a raw-score field in the internal API or the trace.
