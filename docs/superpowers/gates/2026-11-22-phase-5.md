## Fixes found by the gate (c744435)

1. **Readers never long-polled (Critical).**
   - `PeerReplicaSource.awaitChange` read a shard's backoff with `notBefore.getOrDefault(shard, Long.MIN_VALUE)` and then tested `now - nb < 0`. For any positive `System.nanoTime()`, `now - Long.MIN_VALUE` overflows to a negative value.
   - So every shard looked as if it were backing off, no `latest?wait=` was ever issued, and readers picked up new generations only on the refresh pass (10 s here, 15 s by default).
   - Evidence:
     - the run-1 thread dump shows `pharos-replica-longpoll` sleeping in the "no poll" branch (line 588);
     - W1's `/health` showed `longPolls: 0` with readers up, while a hand-made `curl` long-poll was held and counted;
     - in the HEAD run, generation 2 was published at 04:44:12.887 and the reader picked it up at 04:44:18.68, exactly 30 s after its start, that is, on a refresh pass.
   - Fix: an absent entry means no backoff. Test `PeerPullTest.longPollBringsTheNextGenerationLongBeforeTheRefresh` (refresh 3600 s) fails with the old line and passes with the fix.
2. **Publishes from other processes never woke a long-poll (Important).**
   - Only in-process publishes signalled `GenerationSignal`. But the web daemon does not index, so every peer-mode publish comes from a separate `pharos index` or `pharos publish` JVM.
   - The endpoint now re-reads `LATEST` every 0.5 s for the shards with waiting long-polls.
   - Test `ReplicationEndpointTest.publishByAnotherProcessWakesTheLongPoll` fails (timeout) without the watch.
   - After both fixes, publish-to-visible is 0.26–0.6 s; before, it was 5.8–9.8 s.
3. **Counters the gate needed.**
   - Writer `/health` `peers.transfers`: `maxTransfers`, `transfersInFlight`, `peakTransfersInFlight`, `transfersServed`, `transfersRefused`, `bytesServed`, `longPolls`.
   - Reader `peers.peerFetch`: `bytesFromPeers` and `bytesFromWriter`.

The log line and `served()` are misleading during an outage.

**Ruling P5-R11.** The bar stays as it is. If the bucket-mode control on the same host also fails it, or sits within 0.03 of peer mode, the excess counts as host noise. A control ≤ 1.05 that passes while peer mode is > 1.10 would be a regression. Applied here:
- control 1 fails the bar itself.
- Peer runs 1 and 2 pass outright. Their medians, 0.999 and 1.024, sit within 0.03 of the controls' 1.011 and 1.034.
- The p50 ratios overlap: peer 1.018–1.026, control 1.028–1.043.
- No pair shows a passing control ≤ 1.05 against peer > 1.10. peer 0 has no adjacent control; its successor pair, control 1, also fails.
- So the excess in peer 0 and in the local runs is host noise. **Phase 2 warm passes under P5-R11.**
